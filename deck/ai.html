The problem

The answer exists. It's just unreadable when it matters.

1 in 2,000 people have Long QT syndrome. For them an ordinary antibiotic can stop the heart. The risk lists are written for clinicians, by ingredient, in English. At the pharmacy they hold a box called Klacid, and a general chatbot answers fluently, with no guarantee it's right.

Capabilities, limits, control

Every answer is checkable. Every action is yours.

What it can do

  • Name the ingredient behind a brand, a barcode or a photo
  • Give a verdict for 137 QT medicines and their combinations
  • Explain it plainly, by text or voice, in English or Polish
  • Answer offline with a rule-based agent when there's no signal

What it won't do

  • Diagnose, or advise starting or stopping a medicine
  • Read QT or rhythm: a wrist heart rate is not an ECG
  • Call an unknown medicine “safe”: it stays Unknown, grey and dashed
  • Claim full interaction coverage: combo rules are a curated subset

How you verify and stay in control

  • The verdict card is built from data, never from model text
  • Any AI text carries an AI SUMMARY badge
  • Saving a medicine or sharing the card needs your tap; SOS can be cancelled
  • A privacy ledger lists every request that left the phone
