Around 1 in 2,000 people have Long QT syndrome. Their heart takes a little longer to recharge between beats, and an ordinary antibiotic, an anti-sickness pill or a sudden alarm can tip it into a dangerous rhythm.

LQT1

Exertion, especially swimming

A daily tip for your type: warm up, avoid sudden all-out efforts, and never swim alone.

LQT2

Sudden loud sounds

An alarm clock, a slammed door. Celia flags a racing heart while you're at rest and asks if you're OK.

LQT3

Rest and sleep

The risk comes when the heart runs slow. A very low rate at night wakes you with a gentle tone.

Your watch keeps an eye on your heart rate.

Alarms are tuned to your genotype. After you log a risky medicine, the watch watches more closely for 72 hours. If you stop answering, it asks for help.

Heart rate only. Celia.ai never measures QT and never draws an ECG.

Call 112

When you can't explain, the card does.

A paramedic, a pharmacist or a stranger on the tram gets what they need in seconds: the condition, the drugs to avoid, and who to call. Try the languages.

  • SOS that counts down

    Miss a check-in and a 10-second countdown starts. Then your emergency contacts get a message and your location.

  • A guide for bystanders

    Three steps, a call button and a CPR metronome at 110 a minute, like the one beating here, plus a note when there's an ICD.

  • Readable in 13 languages

    Written in advance, never machine-guessed. Abroad, it switches to the local language and emergency number.

  • Shared with a link, sealed on your phone

    Cards and reports are end-to-end encrypted. Only someone holding the full link can read them.

Walk into the appointment with the facts.

Before you see your cardiologist, your GP or your dentist, Celia puts together one page from your own records: medicines and their risk, interactions, your resting heart rate, alerts, symptoms and missed doses, plus the questions worth asking.

Send it as a link that expires in 48 hours, or print it.

  • Offline core

    Medicine checks, the emergency card and your profile live on the phone, encrypted.

  • A ledger of every request

    Each time the phone goes online, it's listed on the phone for you to see.

  • No keys on the phone

    Model keys stay on the backend. Names and contacts are never uploaded in the clear.

  • Deterministic verdicts

    The same medicine always gets the same answer, from data you can trace to a source.
